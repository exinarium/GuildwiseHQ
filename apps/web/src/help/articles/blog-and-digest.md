The Guildwise HQ blog has practical articles on AI agents and AI automation. The articles are written with AI assistance by the blog writer, an automatic job on the Guildwise server, and reviewed before they go public. Every article says so, and the author line is always "The Guildwise HQ team".

## Reading the blog {#reading}

- Inside the app, open **Help** and choose the **Blog** tab. You read the articles without leaving your work, and the search box on the Help centre also finds them under **From the blog**.
- On the public site, go to `/blog`. You can follow the **RSS** feed at `/blog/rss.xml`, and every article has a share link.

## The weekly digest {#digest}

Once a week, on Monday at 08:00 (Johannesburg time), you can get one email with the new articles. In a week with nothing new, nothing is sent.

- **Owners** of an organization get it unless they turn it off.
- **Everyone else** only gets it if they ask for it: tick the box when you accept an invitation, or turn it on in **Settings**.
- People whose organizations are all suspended are not mailed.
- Every digest has an **Unsubscribe** link, and your mail program may show its own unsubscribe button. The link opens a page where you press **Unsubscribe from the weekly digest** to confirm (or **Keep me subscribed**); opening the link alone changes nothing. You do not have to sign in. You can subscribe again from the page that follows, or in Settings.

To change your choice yourself, open **Settings** and use **Weekly blog digest**.

## Running the blog (platform administrator) {#platform-blog}

This part is for the platform administrator. The blog is managed under **Blog** and **API keys** in the platform area.

1. **Writer.** The **Writer** panel on the Blog page runs the blog for you. Switch it on (it is **off** by default) and it writes one or two articles a day (08:00 and 15:00, Johannesburg time): it takes the next topic from your list, researches it on the web, writes the article and sends it into the review queue. It uses the platform's Anthropic key (the one the landing assistant uses), so no workstation or agent is involved. You set:
   - **Articles per day** (1 or 2), the **topics** (one per line; it rotates through them and avoids what the blog already covers), and the **model** (Claude Haiku 4.5 by default).
   - The **monthly cap** in US dollars (default $10). The panel shows this month's spend, the number of runs and the cost of each run (tokens and web searches). Before each article the writer checks that this month's spend plus one more article fits under the cap; if not, it skips the article and you get one email that month.
   - **Write one now** writes an article straight away, even while the writer is off. It respects the cap and the blog kill switch, and shows you the queued article.
   - A model whose price is not known cannot be used until you enter its input and output price, so the cap stays meaningful.

   If a run fails (for example the article did not pass the checks, or the Anthropic service was unreachable) the panel shows the reason under *Last run*; the tokens that were used are still counted.
2. **API keys.** Only needed for outside tools that post through the blog API: create a key with the scopes it needs (`blog:write` is enough to submit posts). The key is shown once. Revoke it at any time. Each request is listed under *Recent requests*.
3. **Review queue.** Every post arrives *In review*. Open it to see it exactly as it will look, edit it, then **Approve and publish**, or **Reject** with a reason (an outside tool sees the reason and can revise; for the built-in writer the reason helps you decide what to change in the topics).
4. **Moderation.** You review every post for the first two weeks. After the date you set under *Auto-publish starts on*, a post that raised no warning publishes by itself once it has waited the *hold* (3 hours by default). Posts that were already waiting when that date arrives are released too, if they raised no warning: they publish after the same hold, counted from that date. Posts with a warning (for example links that are not in the sources) always wait for you.
5. **Kill switch.** *Switch the blog off* stops the writer and API tools creating posts and stops all publishing, the digest and social posts. Public articles stay readable. Switch it on again to carry on.
6. **Weekly digest.** Switch it on or off, send a test to yourself, and see what each week did.
7. **Social sharing.** Connect the Facebook Page, the LinkedIn organization, X and Bluesky. Published articles are shared automatically, with retries, using a caption and hashtags per network (the article summary when a caption is missing). You edit them with a live counter and an exact preview on the post's page; X and Bluesky count the caption and hashtags together. For LinkedIn paste the organization number only, for example 5515715, not the long `urn:li:organization:` form. For X you paste four values from a developer app with "Read and write" access (API key, API secret, access token, access token secret; X charges for API use, so keep credit on the account). For Bluesky you paste the handle and an app password from Settings, App passwords. If a credential is refused, credit runs out or a token is about to expire you get an email, and sharing for that network pauses until you paste new values. Unpublishing an article does not remove what was already shared.
8. **Releases.** The *Releases* tab lists each version of Guildwise HQ from the changelog, with a public page at `/releases/<version>` (what is new, in plain language). **Draft release post** (or **Write by hand**) starts an announcement with captions and hashtags per network. Edit it, send it to review and **Approve and share**: a release post is never posted without your approval, it needs at least one connected network, and each release is announced once. Release posts link to the release page, not to an article.
9. **Videos (YouTube).** The *Videos* tab turns articles, releases and tutorials into short narrated videos and uploads the ones you approve to your YouTube channel. Set it up once: paste the channel's refresh token in **Social sharing** (the YouTube channel row), switch **Videos on**, and run the video worker (the `video` profile of the Docker Compose file; the page says *Video worker not running* when it is missing). Nothing is drafted, rendered or uploaded before that. Nothing is rendered while a script waits for you. The *Videos* tab has three parts. **Review** shows the script cue by cue (the narration, the scene shown while it is spoken and the help-video clips used), the chapters, the title, the description as YouTube will show it, the tags and the hashtags; edit what is wrong. For a Short that goes to more networks there is a caption tab per network with a live character counter (X captions carry no link, and an X post costs $0.015). Press **Schedule: <time>**: it takes the next free slot of your schedule, or pick another free slot in the menu next to it; Shorts and public videos can also be published now. The video is rendered shortly before its slot and goes public on YouTube at the slot. **Preview render** (it says how many of the three renders per video it uses) lets you watch the video first; the player appears only once a render exists. **Queue** shows the next two weeks, a row for videos and one for Shorts: each slot shows the video, where it is in its life and an icon per network, or *Empty* with the number of scripts waiting. Move a video to another free slot, swap two, unschedule it, or retry a failed upload. Past posts are listed below with their links. **Settings** holds the schedule for videos and for Shorts: the days, the time of day, the time zone (Johannesburg by default), where each format is posted (a network that is not connected is greyed out until you connect it under Social sharing) and, under Advanced, how long before the slot the render starts. The writer drafts as many scripts a week as you tick days. If you change the days, videos that sit on days you no longer use are flagged (*N videos sit on slots no longer in your schedule*) and **Re-flow** moves them onto the new slots in order; nothing moves by itself. When a Short goes to **TikTok**, the Schedule step asks TikTok's questions for that post: who can see it (you choose, there is no default), whether people may comment, Duet or Stitch (off unless you tick them), whether the video promotes a brand (*Your brand* or *Branded content*, which cannot be private) and the consent line you must tick. Until the app passes TikTok's audit TikTok posts privately; with no TikTok account connected the step says so and the Short goes to the other networks. The strip at the top shows whether the render and publish workers are online, the next slot of each format and anything that slipped or is waiting for its file. If YouTube keeps an upload private (Google's compliance audit is missing) you see a warning and an email with Google's audit form, and uploads pause until you say you dealt with it. **Library: import help videos** makes a tutorial draft of each existing help video (its recording is uploaded as it is). **Video topics** is a list of ideas for new showcase videos; the writer takes them in order. For a tutorial that is on YouTube you can draft a post for the other networks with the YouTube link and approve it separately. Videos of articles and releases appear on their public pages once they are live.

The blog API for outside tools is described in `docs/BLOG-API.md`.
