# Interview techniques

Use these when you run a discovery conversation.

## One question at a time
Ask one question, then wait. A list of five questions gets one shallow answer. When you need a choice, offer 2 to 4 short
options and always leave room for the person's own words.

## Ask about behaviour, not opinions
"Tell me about the last time this went wrong" gives more than "Would you use a feature that...?". Ask for a recent, real
example, who was there, what they did next, and what it cost them.

## The five whys, lightly
When an answer is a solution ("we need a dashboard"), ask what problem it solves, then what happens if the problem is not
solved. Stop when you reach a cost, a deadline or a person who is hurt. Do not go past three levels in one sitting.

## Reflect, then check
Say back what you heard in one sentence and ask "Is that right?". Correct before you continue. Reflect numbers and names
exactly.

## Separate need from solution
Write down the need ("a sales rep must find a customer's last order in under 10 seconds") apart from the solution
("a search box"). Keep solutions in a parking list until the need is agreed.

## Surface assumptions
When someone says "obviously", "everyone" or "just", ask what they are assuming. Record each assumption with how you would
find out whether it is true.

## Close each topic
End a topic with: what we decided, what is still open, and who owns each open item. Park anything that pulls the
conversation off course instead of dropping it.
